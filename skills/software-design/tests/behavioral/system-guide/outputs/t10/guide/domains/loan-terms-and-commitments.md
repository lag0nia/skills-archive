# Loan Terms And Commitments

This domain makes every part of the system refer to the same loan. It owns the one meaning of a lender's offer, the borrower's reservation of it, and the exact agreed terms that the application, the Base USDC contract, the Bitcoin collateral transactions and the observers all bind to. The [architecture guide](../architecture.md) shows where it fits in the system.

## What this domain does

A Bitcoin-backed USDC loan touches several independent places at once. The borrower and the selected lender see an offer in the product, authorize keys and payout addresses in their own Vaults, move USDC on Base, and prepare Bitcoin transactions that lock and later release the collateral. Each of those artifacts must agree on the participants, the amounts, the deadlines, the payout destinations and the secret commitments. If two components disagreed, one participant could sign for one loan while a contract or transaction enforced another.

The domain solves this by defining one canonical record of the loan and one way of fingerprinting it. A matching fingerprint proves which agreement an artifact describes. It does not authorize a spend, sign anything, move funds or establish what happened on a chain.

The concepts a reader needs:

- **Firm one-loan offer.** A lender publishes an offer for exactly one loan, stating the commercial values: principal, total debt, the repayment deadline, an optional protection expiry and the Bitcoin collateral amount. Borrowers can browse available offers.
- **Reservation.** An authenticated borrower selects one available offer. The selection is atomic: one borrower wins, and any concurrent or stale selection creates nothing. A reservation is identified by the offer and a reservation number that starts at one and increases for each later reservation of the same offer. It binds the actual borrower and lender product accounts, which can never be swapped inside that reservation.
- **Loan identifier.** Reservation creates a stable, cryptographically random 32-byte loan identifier. Every later artifact of this loan carries it. Reopening a cancelled reservation always creates the next reservation number and a fresh loan identifier; an identifier is never reused.
- **Commercial draft.** At reservation the loan has its identifier, participants, commercial values, expiry and cancellation identity; the detailed state records say no canonical agreement exists yet, while the architecture record says reservation creates the canonical loan (an open question described at the end of this guide). Keys, destinations and secret commitments do not exist at that point.
- **Loan terms.** The canonical agreement is a fixed, ordered, versioned record called `LoanTermsV1`. It holds only facts that belong to this one loan and are fixed before the borrower stages collateral: the two participant references, the two Base protocol addresses, the two Base payout recipients, principal, total debt, repayment deadline, optional protection expiry, collateral amount, the two loan-specific Bitcoin public keys, the two external Bitcoin payout destinations, and the commitments to the two secrets described next.
- **Secret commitments.** The borrower's Vault holds a random secret called X; publishing it on Base is how the borrower accepts the loan. The lender's Vault holds a random secret called Y, which becomes public when the lender claims repayment. The terms record carries only their SHA-256 hashes, written H(X) and H(Y), never the secrets. A third secret, S, held by an independent fallback service, appears only for loans created with that separately gated capability; it is not one of the fields of the terms record.
- **Terms hash.** The terms record is encoded with the standard Ethereum contract encoding (the application binary interface, or ABI, encoding) in its fixed field order, prefixed with a versioned domain tag, and hashed with Keccak-256. The result, the terms hash, is the loan's fingerprint. It is a different hash from the SHA-256 secret commitments and is never interchangeable with them.
- **Deployment manifest.** A separate document called `ProtocolDeploymentV1` identifies the environment the loan runs in: the Base chain, the loan contract's proxy and current implementation and its code hash, the USDC token, the Bitcoin network, uniform timing and policy values, and the declared upgrade authority. It deliberately excludes web addresses, provider identities, credentials, application settings, wallets, secrets and anything about an individual loan.

## How its responsibilities work together

The domain has one responsibility, [Loan terms, one-loan offer, reservation, and parameter binding](../../system-model/domains/loan-terms-and-commitments/responsibilities/sr-001-loan-terms-offer-reservation-and-parameter-binding.md). It defines the loan identifier, the terms encoding and hash, the candidate versions and approvals, commitment, cancellation and supersession, and the separation between loan terms and the deployment manifest. It rejects any version, artifact, authority or destination that does not bind exactly.

Because there is only one responsibility, the interesting cooperation is with other domains:

- From [Participant Access And Loan Operations](./participant-access-and-loan-operations.md), the domain receives authenticated participants and their roles, the published offer, the borrower's selection and each participant's approval. The application coordination responsibility there holds the live offer and reservation records and runs the commands; this domain owns what those records mean.
- From the participant Vaults described in [Bitcoin Authorization And Recovery](./bitcoin-authorization-and-recovery.md), it receives each participant's public loan identity (a loan-specific Bitcoin public key and the registered Base address) and each role's prepared payout destinations and secret commitment. The Vaults never send private keys, passwords or secrets.
- To every other domain it provides the committed terms and terms hash. Bitcoin package construction binds its transaction packages and their manifests to the loan identifier, terms version and terms hash. The Base contract described in [Base USDC Settlement And Funding](./base-usdc-settlement-and-funding.md) receives the complete terms when the funding window opens and recomputes the hash itself. The observers in [Base Observation And Fallback Review](./base-observation-and-fallback-review.md) reject evidence from the wrong deployment.
- To Base, Bitcoin, Vault, application, worker and build consumers it provides the pinned deployment manifest and its fingerprint.

## A normal case

The following case is illustrative; the names and amounts are not approved requirements.

Lena, a lender, publishes a firm offer: 10,000 USDC principal, 10,300 USDC total debt, a fixed repayment deadline, no protection, and 0.30 BTC collateral. Publishing is the lender's standing acceptance of the offer; whether it is also the lender's last approval is the open question described below. Bruno, a borrower, browses offers and selects hers. The application atomically reserves the offer for Bruno, records both product accounts, creates the stable loan identifier and stores the commercial draft with a 24-hour reservation expiry. In the detailed records no canonical agreement exists yet, and nothing has moved on any chain.

Next, each participant's Vault does two things. The Vault is a separately hosted application; the participant's keys and secrets are stored only encrypted and are decrypted and used only in that participant's browser. First it allocates a loan-specific Bitcoin key for this loan and returns only the public key together with the participant's already registered Base protocol address. Then it confirms that role's payout destinations (for Bruno, his Base recipient and his external Bitcoin address; for Lena, her Base settlement recipient and her external Bitcoin address), creates and encrypts that role's secret only if it does not already exist, and returns only the commitment H(X) or H(Y). Only when both allocation results and both preparation results are durably stored does the application service assemble one complete candidate: a version number, the full terms and the exact terms hash.

The product then shows each participant the understandable candidate terms and both bound public identities. In the detailed records, Bruno and Lena each approve that exact candidate version and hash through an authenticated command, and the second matching approval creates the canonical agreement exactly once. Whether Lena must give this second approval is an open question: other records say reservation needs no second lender approval (see [Open, excluded and deferred](#open-excluded-and-deferred)).

After commitment the reservation is still open. Bruno now has to stage his Bitcoin collateral, and Bitcoin packages and the Base funding window are built on the committed terms. The reservation is consumed only when Bruno's acceptance on Base, which makes X public, reaches finality.

```figure
caption: From published offer to committed terms. Nothing moves on either chain during these steps, and the approval step carries an open question.
step: Publish | The lender publishes a firm one-loan offer with its commercial values.
step: Reserve | The borrower atomically reserves it; a stable loan identifier and a commercial draft are created.
step: Allocate | Each Vault allocates a loan key and returns only public identities.
step: Prepare | Each Vault confirms its role's destinations and returns its secret commitment.
step: Assemble | The application assembles one complete candidate version and terms hash.
step: Approve | Detailed records require both roles to approve that candidate; others say no second lender approval (open).
step: Commit | The second matching approval commits the terms exactly once.
```

## What else is fixed: the environment

The deployment manifest answers a different question from the terms: not "which loan" but "which system". There is exactly one manifest per complete environment:

- **production:** Base mainnet, native Circle USDC and Bitcoin mainnet;
- **test:** Base Sepolia, Circle's test USDC and the default Bitcoin Signet;
- **local:** a local Base development chain, a locally deployed mock USDC and Bitcoin regtest.

All consumers ship with the same file from one pinned release of the shared protocol library; nothing fetches it at run time. Its fingerprint is SHA-256 over the strictly parsed document with keys sorted recursively and the fingerprint field itself left out, written as `sha256:` followed by lowercase hexadecimal. Every consumer recomputes the fingerprint before use. Base-facing consumers also check the live chain, proxy address, implementation address and code hash, loan-contract configuration, and USDC identity; Bitcoin-facing consumers check the configured network. The application stores the originating library version and manifest fingerprint with each loan, so a later release never silently retargets an existing loan. A manifest changes only through a new, explicitly pinned library release.

A configured address in the manifest is not proof of deployed code, ownership, finality or a successful transaction, and the manifest does not authorize an upgrade, open a funding window, sponsor gas or change an existing loan.

Base addresses deserve one note. An address is canonically its 20-byte value, not its text. The normal spelling uses the Ethereum mixed-case checksum convention (EIP-55), and the Vault's registered identity keeps that exact spelling end to end. For compatibility with already published fixtures, the library also accepts an all-lowercase address; any mixed-case spelling must have a valid checksum. Both accepted spellings encode to identical bytes and the same terms hash, and equality is always by value.

## Exceptions and recovery

**Lost races and retries.** A concurrent or stale reservation loses without creating a second reservation or loan. Retrying a reservation with the same idempotency key and body returns the same result after a timeout or restart; reusing the key with a changed body is rejected. Stale, wrong-role, wrong-loan or mismatched approvals, and approvals for an expired or cancelled draft, are rejected. Duplicate identical approvals and commitment retries return the same durable record, and two concurrent final approvals commit at most once. This is what prevents an apparently successful retry from reviving an old approval.

**Changed terms.** Any change to any field that enters the terms hash produces a new candidate version and hash and invalidates every earlier approval. Choosing a Vault payment address as a recipient on a fresh loan is such a value change: the address must first be qualified as recoverable, the choice needs fresh approvals, and it never rewrites the terms of an earlier loan. Before activation, an amendment creates a later version with two fresh approvals and fresh dependent artifacts, and the earlier version becomes superseded history; whether the lender's approval here is a separate final-terms approval is undecided (see [Open, excluded and deferred](#open-excluded-and-deferred)). An active escrow or Base loan is never amended in place.

**Expiry, cancellation and reopening.** These depend on whether terms were committed:

- If the 24-hour reservation passes before terms are committed and without a prefund attempt, the reservation expires; no loan or second lender approval may be inferred.
- Either bound participant can cancel a draft or a candidate awaiting approval, and expiry before commitment cancels it too. Cancellation is immutable and idempotent, keeps the history, invalidates pending approvals and blocks commitment and package construction. Cancellation is terminal for that reservation.
- After commitment, if no valid prefund (the borrower's staged collateral transaction) is visible by the 24-hour deadline, the application cancels the reservation, reopens the offer, permanently blocks that borrower from the same offer, adds exactly one infraction and notifies both parties.
- A valid prefund seen in the Bitcoin mempool by the deadline keeps the reservation but proves nothing about collateral yet. If it stays unconfirmed for 72 hours, the reservation moves into manual review: operations are alerted and the borrower is emailed to raise the fee; this is not cancellation, an infraction or activation. Operations may manually cancel and reopen the offer when the transaction cannot progress or appears abusive; a valid stuck transaction carries no automatic infraction.
- Reopening always creates the next reservation number and a fresh loan identifier. A cancelled record is never reversed, deleted or reassigned.

Failures before X is public lead to the pre-activation unwind described in [Base USDC Settlement And Funding](./base-usdc-settlement-and-funding.md) and [Bitcoin Authorization And Recovery](./bitcoin-authorization-and-recovery.md).

**Anything that does not bind.** Every consumer rejects an unsupported encoding or version, a changed fingerprint, the wrong chain, contract, asset or network, a mixed environment, a wrong key, destination or commitment, and any dependent artifact that does not carry or resolve the exact loan identifier, terms version, terms hash and relevant deployment and package identity. Such an artifact is not part of the loan. No mutable copy, acceptance snapshot, Base record, package label or observer view may repair or reinterpret it. A deployment mismatch blocks the value-moving or lifecycle action and records the discrepancy for reconciliation.

> **Residual risk: substitution before approval**
> The binding protects the agreement after it is approved, not a participant deceived into approving the wrong values. If a compromised interface or backend substitutes a destination, recipient, public key, contract, amount, deadline or commitment before commitment, and the participant approves it, later fixed transactions faithfully enforce the substitution. The required control is to show the exact human-readable recipients, amounts, chains, deadlines, key fingerprints, terms version and hash, and deployment identity at every signing or value-moving boundary, and to compare wallet calls and Bitcoin transaction bytes against the committed terms rather than against what the interface shows. The [substitution threat record](../../system-model/contracts/security/sec-012-term-destination-public-key-or-contract-substitution.md) owns this.

## Authority boundaries

This domain decides what the loan means. It does not decide or imply anything else:

- **Approval is not execution.** Approving terms grants no Vault, signing, secret, transaction, funding or custody authority, and a committed agreement is not a Bitcoin signature, a Base transaction or a chain observation. Reservation creates no Bitcoin signature, prefund, Base funding, wallet unlock, key access or secret disclosure.
- **The lender keeps its capital decision.** Even after terms are committed, the selected lender alone decides whether, when and from which of its wallets to transfer USDC for funding. The product never pools, holds or automatically deploys lender capital.
- **Identities stay separate.** A participant's loan-specific Bitcoin public key, generated Base protocol address, independent Vault payment account, external Bitcoin payout destination, fixed Base payout recipient and repayment-refund recipient are separate fields with separate authority. The payment account may pay an eligible transfer, but paying from it does not change any recipient fixed in the terms, and it never silently becomes a recipient or protocol caller. A protocol key or Base protocol address never silently becomes a payout destination, and no organization key may replace a participant's protocol key.
- **Copies are not authority.** Workspace views, acceptance snapshots, display copies and email snapshots are presentations or audit data. They cannot redefine committed terms or act as chain authority.
- **Other domains keep their jobs.** Product sign-in and roles, Vault decryption and signing, Bitcoin construction, Base settlement, observation and workflow coordination remain with their own domains.

## Choices and their reasons

- **Loan facts and environment facts are kept apart.** The terms hold only per-loan facts; values repeated for every loan, such as the chain and contract, live in the deployment manifest. The user specified that repeated values should not be stored per loan, and approved the per-loan field list the agent recommended. Separately maintained component configurations would permit a cross-domain mismatch that is hard to detect after funds move, so one manifest per environment was chosen; the user approved it on the agent's recommendation, while the agent chose the fingerprint algorithm and release rule without presenting them for approval.
- **One canonical encoding and hash that Base recomputes.** The recorded rationale is interoperability and verifiability: divergent encodings, or mixing loan and environment facts, could bind signatures, manifests and chain actions to different loans or make a package impossible to reproduce. A database-only identifier would not tie Base state to the terms used for Bitcoin packages. The user approved the Keccak-256 hash that the contract recomputes, on the agent's recommendation.
- **Addresses, not scripts, for Bitcoin destinations.** The user specified that destinations are the participants' own external addresses and that any valid address for the network is acceptable; on the agent's recommendation, which the user approved, output scripts are derived internally and the raw collateral scripts are kept with the Bitcoin packages rather than in the terms.
- **A fixed repayment deadline with a cushion.** The user specified a fixed deadline set before origination with a 24-hour commercial cushion, so everything is set up at the start; the cushion is not a separate field and the deadline does not move if setup takes longer. Any protection expiry must be at least seven days after the repayment deadline, as the user specified.
- **Versioned candidates, two approvals, idempotent retries.** Dependent setup must never act on an unaccepted, mutated, cancelled or ambiguously retried agreement. The user approved this lifecycle on the agent's recommendation. Whether the lender's part in it is a separate final-terms approval, or is already given by publishing the offer, is undecided (see [Open, excluded and deferred](#open-excluded-and-deferred)).
- **One offer, one loan.** One offer for one loan makes reservation atomic and understandable; a pooled multi-loan listing would need allocation and concurrency rules V1 does not need, and a per-offer counter keeps history when an offer returns. The user specified that there is only one lender per loan and that V1 must not be described as public or multi-lender matching, pooled capital, custody or automatic capital deployment.
- **Addresses compared by value.** Treating the 20-byte value as canonical preserves the Vault's exact identity, keeps existing fixtures valid, matches the contract's own address semantics and fails closed on a corrupted checksum. The user approved this repair on the agent's recommendation.
- **Extensions as successor loans.** A future extension would be a new loan with fresh identifier, terms, Base entry, escrow, secrets and packages, linked only off-chain, because mutating an active loan or reusing its secrets and packages would create unsafe coupling. The user approved this on the agent's recommendation; no extension is implemented.

The [domain record](../../system-model/domains/loan-terms-and-commitments/domain.md) links the record that owns each choice and its attribution.

## Open, excluded and deferred

**Open: does the lender approve twice?** The records disagree, and the package has not decided. On one side, the [architecture record](../../system-model/architecture.md) says publication is the lender's standing acceptance and that reservation creates the canonical loan without a second lender approval; the [offer and reservation contract](../../system-model/contracts/interfaces/iface-003-one-loan-offer-reservation-and-loan-binding.md), the [one-loan offer selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-012-one-loan-offer-reservation-and-binding.md) and the [excluded-scope record](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-011-public-multi-lender-pooling-custody-and-automatic-capital.md) also say there is no second lender approval, the [origination flow](../../system-model/behavior/flows/flow-001-origination-preparation-and-qualification.md) calls publication standing acceptance, and the [funding vault state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-010-base-funding-vault.md) lists a "second lender approval" among its invalid transitions. On the other side, the responsibility record, the [canonical terms state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-002-canonical-loan-terms.md), the origination flow's approval step and the [offer and reservation state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-001-one-loan-offer-and-reservation.md) require both borrower and lender to approve the same final candidate before commitment, and the offer and reservation state says the lender's final-terms approval is distinct from its earlier approval of the commercial offer. The responsibility record also says publication approves the proposed commercial values only, which could reconcile the reservation wording with a later final-terms approval, but not the one-loan offer selection's statement that there is no second lender-approval step. Until this is answered, read every statement in this guide about the lender's approval as describing the detailed records, with this question open.

**Undecided: cancelling committed terms.** The terms lifecycle decision says either participant can cancel before any Bitcoin or USDC action. The canonical terms state lists participant cancellation only from the draft and awaiting-approval stages. The records do not say whether a participant can still cancel after commitment but before the prefund; the no-prefund rule above still applies.

**Naming difference.** The responsibility record lists a separate awaiting-allocations stage and calls the approval stage awaiting final approvals; the canonical terms state treats waiting for allocations as part of the commercial draft and calls the approval stage awaiting acceptances. The behavior described here is the same in both.

**Excluded from V1.** Public or multi-lender matching, pooled lender capital, product custody and automatic capital deployment are excluded, because they would change offer allocation, funding authority, custody, accounting, security and likely legal boundaries. BTC liquidation, automated surplus return and shortfall enforcement are excluded under a [separate scope record](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-012-bitcoin-liquidation-surplus-and-shortfall.md); a protection expiry is not liquidation.

**Deferred.** Protection or put-option operations are deferred: V1 commits only the optional protection expiry, and provider selection, exercise and settlement wait for a later design. Immutable conversion of the Base contract and routing to a successor deployment are deferred; V1 manifests declare the active upgrade authority and do not claim it is frozen. Loan extensions are not implemented. The independent fallback secret S, and its commitment, are unavailable for production until counsel approval and implementation evidence activate that capability.

**Delivery status now.** The delivery record lists the earlier library and application slices as implemented. The destination-and-secret preparation step and the final-approval gating that depends on it are a planned application-service change that has not been implemented. The Vault and product steps that consume it, and then the cross-domain acceptance evidence, must follow before the runnable local demonstration is unblocked. The library release that would carry the real environment manifests, testnet and mainnet deployment, and production operation are separate later gates; nothing here claims them. The [handoff sequence](../../build/workflow/handoffs/README.md) holds the current status.

The [domain record](../../system-model/domains/loan-terms-and-commitments/domain.md) lists exactly what the domain owns and excludes; the [terms contract](../../system-model/contracts/interfaces/iface-001-loan-terms-and-canonical-binding.md) and the [deployment manifest contract](../../system-model/contracts/interfaces/iface-002-protocol-deployment-manifest.md) hold the exact fields and encodings.
