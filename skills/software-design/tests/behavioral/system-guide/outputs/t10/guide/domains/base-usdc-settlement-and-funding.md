# Base USDC Settlement And Funding

This domain handles every movement of the loan's USDC on Base: it takes in the lender's principal, pays it to the borrower when the borrower accepts the loan, takes in the repayment, and pays that repayment to exactly one side. It matters because these movements follow fixed contract rules that no participant, payer, operator or application can redirect. The [architecture guide](../architecture.md) shows where it fits in the system.

## What this domain does

A loan in this system pairs Bitcoin collateral with a USDC loan between one borrower and one selected lender. Base is a layer-2 network that settles on Ethereum, and USDC here means the configured native USDC token on Base. This domain owns the Base side of the loan: one authoritative on-chain loan record, fixed rules for funding, the borrower's acceptance, repayment, the lender's claim and every refund, and the lender-facing step in which the lender actually sends capital.

It does not own the Bitcoin side. The collateral lives under Bitcoin's own consensus rules, handled by the [Bitcoin Authorization And Recovery](./bitcoin-authorization-and-recovery.md) domain. A Base payout can make a secret public, and that secret can let a Bitcoin transaction the participants signed in advance become complete. A Base event cannot sign, broadcast or confirm any Bitcoin transaction, and a Base refund is not a Bitcoin recovery.

A few concepts recur throughout:

- The **main contract** is the Base contract that keeps one record per loan: the hash of the agreed terms, the fixed recipients, principal and debt, the hashes of the secrets X and Y, the deadlines and the loan's state. Every successful action emits exactly one named **event**, and that event history is the source the rest of the system reads.
- The **funding vault** is a small immutable contract, deployed for one loan when its funding window opens, that receives the lender's principal. The **repayment vault** is a second, separate immutable contract, deployed for the same loan at the moment the borrower accepts, that receives the repayment. Neither vault has an owner, administrator, upgrade path, arbitrary call, asset choice, destination choice or general token-rescue function.
- USDC sitting in a vault is **provisional**. It counts only when a named main-contract action moves the exact required amount, and that action's event reaches Base L1 finality.
- **Base L1 finality** is the point at which the Base block holding an event can no longer be reversed, because it is final on Ethereum (layer 1). Before that point an event is progress information only. The [Base Observation And Fallback Review](./base-observation-and-fallback-review.md) domain decides when an event has reached it.
- **Payer-neutral** means that anyone may send USDC to either vault. Sending tokens does not make the sender the borrower or the lender, does not change any recipient and gives the sender no refund right.
- **Fixed recipients** are the addresses fixed by the approved loan terms and the contract state, never by whoever pays or calls: the borrower's principal recipient, the lender's settlement recipient (which also receives every refund before acceptance), and the borrower's repayment-refund recipient.
- **Permissionless** actions can be submitted by any address. The caller pays the transaction fee (gas) and chooses nothing about amount, recipient or meaning. Only two actions are restricted to one caller: opening the funding window, and the borrower's one-time switch of the repayment-refund recipient.
- **X** and **Y** are secrets whose hashes are fixed into each loan. The borrower reveals X on Base to accept the loan; the lender reveals Y on Base to claim the repayment. Once public, each lets one matching pre-signed Bitcoin transaction be completed.
- **D** is the loan's immutable repayment deadline. **C**, the lender's claim cutoff, is D plus 172,800 seconds (48 hours). **T**, D plus 259,200 seconds (72 hours), is the earliest time the lender's Bitcoin default transaction can become valid; T is measured by Bitcoin consensus time, while every Base deadline uses Base contract time.

## How its responsibilities work together

[Base loan state and USDC settlement](../../system-model/domains/base-usdc-settlement-and-funding/responsibilities/sr-003-base-loan-state-and-usdc-settlement.md) is the contract side. It owns the loan record, both vaults, the deadlines, the exact amounts, the fixed recipients, the named events and errors, and every value-moving action: opening the funding window, finalizing funding, acceptance with X, finalizing repayment, the lender's claim with Y, the refund-recipient switch, the expiry and refund actions, and the sweeps of extra USDC. It also owns the contract's administration boundary: how the main contract may be upgraded and how the account that opens funding windows can be replaced. The exact function surface, events and errors are in the [Base transition interface](../../system-model/contracts/interfaces/iface-009-base-transition-api-events-and-errors.md).

[Lender-controlled funding-vault interaction](../../system-model/domains/base-usdc-settlement-and-funding/responsibilities/sr-005-lender-controlled-funding-vault-interaction.md) is the human side of funding. After the finalized window-opening event identifies the deployed vault, it shows the lender the verified chain, contract, native USDC, loan, principal still missing, deadline and the fixed lender settlement recipient. The lender then decides whether, when and from which wallet or treasury it controls to send USDC, in one transfer or several; partial deposits add up. This responsibility shows the provisional balance and coordinates the permissionless finalization and recovery calls, but signing stays with the lender's own wallet and only finalized main-contract events advance the loan. It never pools, holds, substitutes or automatically draws lender capital.

The two cooperate through the funding vault: the interaction presents it and gathers the lender's transfers, and the contract decides when exactly the principal has arrived. Repayment uses the same pattern, but the guided repayment screens belong to the application rather than to this domain.

The domain receives the committed loan terms and the approved deployment description from the [Loan Terms And Commitments](./loan-terms-and-commitments.md) domain. Participants' Vaults, described in the [Participant Access And Loan Operations](./participant-access-and-loan-operations.md) and Bitcoin guides, submit the secret-bearing calls: the borrower's acceptance with X, the lender's claim with Y, and the refund-recipient switch. The application in the Participant Access And Loan Operations domain schedules and submits the permissionless calls, usually from a non-privileged organization fee wallet, and shows participants what is happening. In return, this domain emits the named events that the Base Observation And Fallback Review domain qualifies to finality and passes on to the application and to Bitcoin operations.

A participant's Vault may also hold a separate **Vault payment account**: a Base account whose key the participant's Vault keeps, which can be one eligible payer for funding or repayment. The design allows it only after the participant's encrypted Vault backup can restore it, and each payment needs an exact, reviewed transfer request. Using it changes nothing in the contracts: they stay payer-neutral, and the account does not become a protocol caller or alter any recipient or any historical loan terms. A fresh loan may use the account's address as one of its existing recipient values only through the normal approval of that loan's terms. The [Vault payment account interface](../../system-model/contracts/interfaces/iface-022-vault-owned-base-payment-account-and-exact-transfer.md) records that the Vault's signing for these payment transfers is planned and not yet verified.

## A normal case: one loan from funding to lender claim

The amounts and wallets below are illustrative. Suppose the loan's principal is 10,000 USDC and its debt is 10,300 USDC.

Before Base is involved, the borrower's Bitcoin collateral is staged and the participants sign their Bitcoin transactions in advance. When the Bitcoin side qualifies (after six unchanged confirmations of the borrower's staged Bitcoin, and with at least 67 hours left before that staged Bitcoin can be refunded), a dedicated organization account called the **opener** creates the loan record and its funding vault. The vault exists before anyone sees its address, and a 24-hour funding window starts.

The lender sees the vault and sends 6,000 USDC from its treasury, then 4,050 USDC from another wallet it controls. The vault now holds 10,050 USDC, but nothing is funded yet. Any caller, normally the application's worker, then finalizes funding: the main contract moves exactly 10,000 USDC into itself and leaves the extra 50 USDC in the vault. Funding finalization starts a 24-hour acceptance deadline for the borrower; once that event is final, the loan counts as funded but not accepted.

The borrower accepts by revealing X through its own Vault, which signs and submits the call from the borrower's committed Base account. The borrower's side allows this only while a fresh readiness check passes, including at least 19 hours remaining before the staged Bitcoin becomes refundable. In one indivisible action the contract pays exactly 10,000 USDC to the borrower's fixed principal recipient, deploys and registers the repayment vault, and makes X public. If any part fails, nothing happens. Acceptance does not make the collateral active: the Bitcoin side must still confirm its activation transaction under its own rules.

Before showing the repayment vault, the application displays the borrower's current repayment-refund recipient and asks the borrower to confirm it or to use the one-time switch. The borrower then sends 7,000 USDC, and a friend's wallet sends the remaining 3,300 USDC; the friend gains no role or right by doing so. The guided transfer closes one hour before D. No later than D, any caller finalizes repayment: exactly 10,300 USDC moves into the main contract, and the `Repaid` event, once final, establishes valid repayment.

Before C, the lender claims by revealing Y from its own Vault. The contract pays the 10,300 USDC once to the lender's fixed settlement recipient and makes Y public. The observation domain qualifies that event, and Bitcoin operations can then complete the borrower's pre-signed recovery transaction. Since funding was finalized, the leftover 50 USDC in the funding vault can be swept at any time, by anyone, but only to the lender's settlement recipient.

```figure
caption: Each step counts only when its event reaches Base L1 finality; vault balances alone prove nothing.
step: Window opens | After Bitcoin qualification, the opener creates the loan record and its funding vault.
step: Lender funds | The lender sends USDC in ordinary transfers from any wallet it controls; the balance is provisional.
step: Funding finalized | Any caller moves exactly the principal into the main contract; the 24-hour acceptance window starts.
step: Borrower accepts | Revealing X pays the principal, creates the repayment vault and makes X public, all at once.
step: Borrower repays | Any wallet sends USDC to the repayment vault; the guided transfer closes one hour before D.
step: Repayment finalized | No later than D, any caller moves exactly the debt into the main contract.
step: Lender claims | Strictly before C, revealing Y pays the debt to the lender's recipient and makes Y public.
```

The exact states and transitions are in the [main Base loan state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-011-main-base-loan.md), the [activation flow](../../system-model/behavior/flows/flow-002-activation-completion-and-active-entry.md) and the [normal repayment flow](../../system-model/behavior/flows/flow-004-normal-repayment-close.md).

## When funding or acceptance does not happen

If the funding window ends without finalized funding, any caller can mark the attempt expired, which returns the funding vault's current USDC balance only to the lender's fixed settlement recipient. A finalization that was submitted but not finalized before the deadline does not win. If funding was finalized but the borrower does not accept within 24 hours, any caller can refund exactly the principal, once, to that same lender recipient.

Only a holder of X can accept, and X stays in the borrower's Vault until the borrower reveals it. The organization holds no copy of X, and there is no delegated or automatic acceptance; that recovery route is deliberately [deferred](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-003-borrower-nonresponse-activation-recovery.md). The borrower's staged Bitcoin follows its own prefund refund route in the Bitcoin domain, so the lender's USDC and the borrower's Bitcoin are recovered separately. The [preactivation unwind flow](../../system-model/behavior/flows/flow-003-preactivation-unwind.md) owns that sequence. Once X is final on Base, this unwind is no longer available, and the Bitcoin side must continue the same activation.

## When repayment is missing, partial or late

Valid repayment exists only when the exact debt in native USDC, held in the loan's registered repayment vault, is finalized no later than D and the `Repaid` event reaches Base L1 finality. A sufficient balance is necessary but is not repayment. Partial transfers, transfers to the wrong vault or chain, a submitted finalization, or the identity of the payer prove nothing. The [valid repayment rule](../../system-model/contracts/invariants/inv-006-valid-repayment.md) states this exactly.

The application's guided transfer stops one hour before D to leave time for finalization, and warns that a later direct transfer may not finalize by D. The vault itself still accepts transfers after that. If D passes without finalized repayment, the USDC in the repayment vault (a partial payment, or a late one) can be swept by anyone only to the borrower's current repayment-refund recipient. The sweep does not cure the missed repayment. The application then classifies ordinary non-payment, and the lender's Bitcoin default route can become valid only at T under Bitcoin's own rules, handled in the Bitcoin domain.

## When the lender does not claim

The lender's claim and the borrower's refund of a valid repayment are mutually exclusive, separated by the cutoff C:

- Strictly before C, the lender can claim with the matching Y. The claim pays the lender's fixed settlement recipient once and makes Y public.
- At C, the claim becomes permanently impossible, and the borrower's current repayment-refund recipient is frozen.
- From C on, anyone can refund the exact unclaimed debt, once, to that frozen recipient. The refund right never expires. The application schedules it after `Repaid` is final and normally submits it from the non-privileged fee wallet, but if another caller executes first, that also counts as success.

The **repayment-refund recipient** starts as the borrower's fixed principal recipient. Strictly before C, whether before or after repayment, only the borrower's committed Base protocol account (the externally owned account, or EOA, derived in the borrower's Vault and fixed in the terms) can switch it, once and irreversibly, to that same account. No one can choose any other address. This lets a borrower who has lost access to the original recipient still receive a refund.

The finalized refund event, `UnclaimedRepaymentRefunded`, is the only Base fact that can trigger the optional Bitcoin fallback described in the Base Observation And Fallback Review guide, and only for a loan created after that fallback's production activation with its secret committed. Lender silence, a missing Y, elapsed time, a `Repaid` event or raw token activity cannot trigger it. The Base refund never depends on that fallback. It also does not return the borrower's Bitcoin: the collateral can remain in escrow until one of these confirms on Bitcoin: a recovery completed with Y or an activated fallback, a cooperative settlement, the lender's default transaction (valid from T) or, for newly constructed escrows only, the borrower's long-stop recovery (valid from D plus 864,000 seconds, 10 days, without Y or the fallback secret). Escrows built before that long-stop route was added cannot gain it. The [residual-risk record](../../system-model/contracts/security/sec-024-valid-repayment-plus-missing-y-leaves-bitcoin-unsettled.md) describes that outcome, and the [claim and refund exclusivity rule](../../system-model/contracts/invariants/inv-007-claim-refund-exclusivity-and-fallback-trigger.md) and the [repayment-refund recipient state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-013-repayment-refund-recipient-and-claim-cutoff.md) give the exact conditions.

## Extra and late USDC

USDC that exceeds the required amount never blocks finalization; it stays in the vault. Each vault has one fixed recovery destination:

| Vault | When anyone may sweep its USDC | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Funding vault | After funding is finalized or after the funding window expires, including USDC that arrives later | The lender's fixed settlement recipient |
| Repayment vault | After repayment is finalized, or strictly after D, including USDC that arrives later | The borrower's current repayment-refund recipient, frozen at C |

Only the configured USDC is recoverable. Any other token sent to a vault is unsupported and may remain stuck, because the design deliberately has no general rescue function and no administrator recovery.

## Failed calls, retries and what Base evidence cannot prove

Every wrong, duplicate, early, late, underfunded, unauthorized or conflicting call reverts with a named error: no state changes, no success event is emitted and no USDC moves. A call that was simulated, submitted, timed out or reported complete by a worker is not a transition. The application must reconcile the actual transaction and event before retrying, and a retry keeps the same fixed effect. The permissionless recovery calls remain callable indefinitely, so a failure of the application's automation never removes them.

If the organization's whole hosting provider is down, the chains continue and the application claims nothing. The contract's valid exits, such as a refund or a sweep, can still be called directly by anyone, and the application reconciles Base and Bitcoin when it returns.

Some risks remain, and the records state them plainly:

- **Acceptance and Bitcoin activation are not one atomic step.** Revealing X pays the principal on Base, but the Bitcoin activation may still fail to confirm before the borrower's staged Bitcoin becomes refundable. If the refund confirms first, the borrower holds the principal without locked collateral and the lender is left with unsecured credit exposure. The records call this the principal residual cross-chain risk; see [Base pays principal but activation does not confirm](../../system-model/contracts/security/sec-023-base-pays-principal-but-bitcoin-activation-does-not-confirm.md).
- **A contract defect can affect every funded loan in the deployment.** Bitcoin's pre-signed transactions cannot recover stolen USDC or make a false Base transition true; see the [contract exploit record](../../system-model/contracts/security/sec-016-base-contract-accounting-or-transition-exploit.md).
- **Upgrade authority can be economically equivalent to custody.** Because the main contract is upgradeable, a compromise of its governance could redirect or lock USDC across many loans. During the intentionally short public mainnet beta, one dedicated key alone holds that upgrade authority, before the same sole ownership moves to the Safe-controlled timelock. Whether that fits the records is unclear: the [deployment and upgrade state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-019-base-deployment-upgrade-and-opener.md) lists an unrestricted unilateral upgrade or administration key as invalid, and the [upgrade boundary rule](../../system-model/contracts/invariants/inv-020-base-upgrade-boundary.md) describes only the Safe and timelock; no record says how either applies to the beta key. The practical claim that the organization cannot redirect Base funds is only as strong as that administration model; see the [upgrade compromise record](../../system-model/contracts/security/sec-017-base-upgrade-administrator-or-operational-signer-compromise.md).
- **A deceptive screen can still mislead a payer.** A transfer sent to the wrong address or chain creates no funding or repayment. The application must display the exact chain, deployment, vault, token, amount, deadline and fixed recovery recipient.

## Authority boundaries

This domain decides the Base outcome of a loan: whether funding, acceptance, repayment, the lender's claim, a refund or a sweep happened, for exactly which amount, to exactly which fixed recipient. It decides nothing else.

> **Holding a secret or paying gas is not authority**
> Revealing X or Y enables only that call's fixed effect. A permissionless caller, a payer, the opener, the organization's fee wallet and a workforce operator choose no amount, recipient or outcome. An operator can only request an operation that is already fixed, under the [operator authorization rule](../../build/records/constraints/cons-019-workforce-operator-authorization-and-audit.md).

- **The lender alone controls capital.** The lender decides whether, when and from which controlled wallet or treasury to send principal. The product never pools, holds or automatically deploys lender capital, and never sells Bitcoin; see the [selected-lender capital rule](../../system-model/contracts/invariants/inv-011-selected-lender-capital-control.md).
- **The opener only opens.** It may open a funding window for a loan the application has committed, after the Bitcoin and timing checks. It cannot move funds or change terms. An attacker holding only the opener key could create junk records under invented loan numbers at its own cost; the application quarantines any opened loan it did not commit and never treats it as a product loan.
- **The contract does not judge evidence.** The claim cutoff and the refund depend on contract time and state alone, with no review of evidence and no veto by the fallback service or the product.
- **This domain does not own** Bitcoin activation or any Bitcoin outcome, participants' passwords, keys or unrevealed secrets, the meaning of the loan terms, the finality classification of events, or the application's displayed status.

## Choices and their reasons

- **One deployed, immutable funding vault per loan.** It lets the lender send principal with an ordinary transfer from any wallet, without connecting that wallet to the application or building contract calls, and every transfer has an unambiguous on-chain destination. Alternatives were rejected because a direct funding call needs calldata and a token approval, a not-yet-deployed address could strand funds if deployment failed, and a shared deposit address would make routing ambiguous. The records show the user asked for funding from any wallet and chose the per-loan vault; see the [funding vault selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-021-immutable-per-loan-funding-vault.md).
- **A separate repayment vault, created inside the acceptance.** Any wallet can repay by ordinary transfer, partial payments are attributable to one loan, and no payer approves a token allowance. Creating it in the same action as acceptance avoids a deployment race, and keeping it separate from the funding vault keeps two different recovery policies apart. The user asked to reuse the per-loan vault pattern for repayment; see the [repayment vault selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-022-immutable-per-loan-repayment-vault.md).
- **An upgradeable main contract under delayed multisignature control.** The selected path keeps a governed repair route during the early beta, with public code and an automated compatibility check before every upgrade and a public seven-day delay. One dedicated key controls local and test use and an intentionally short public mainnet beta; ownership then moves to a 2-of-3 Safe (a multisignature account) that controls the timelock. A compatible upgrade becomes the one code path for every loan held by the contract, must preserve fixed recipients and lifecycle authority, and does not move existing loans to another contract. Immutable code from day one was rejected because it gives no repair path. The user chose the single beta key and one rule for all loans; the Safe, the 2-of-3 threshold and the seven-day delay were agent recommendations the user approved. See the [upgrade governance selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-009-base-contract-upgrade-governance.md).
- **No on-chain pause.** During an incident the organization stops its frontend, its API and the opener; it adds no second on-chain administrator. This stop cannot prevent a direct valid contract call and does not stop an existing loan's funding, repayment, refund or release. A lost or compromised opener is replaced only through the published, compatibility-checked, seven-day-delayed upgrade path. The user rejected an on-chain stop switch, saying stopping belongs to the API. See the [opening authority constraint](../../build/records/constraints/cons-008-base-window-opening-authority.md). The deployment and upgrade state, however, lists pause and operational roles and a "paused for new or risky action" state, so whether V1 has any on-chain pause role is undecided (see [the open section](#open-excluded-and-deferred)).
- **A 48-hour claim window, then a permanent borrower refund.** A valid repayment is not kept locked indefinitely. The user set the 48 hours and noted that keeping the USDC there is itself what is dangerous; any wallet may then execute the refund. See the [unclaimed repayment constraint](../../build/records/constraints/cons-010-unclaimed-repayment-exclusivity.md).

## Open, excluded and deferred

**Open: how the lender approves the final terms.** The records disagree, and the question is the user's to decide. On one side, the [architecture record](../../system-model/architecture.md) and the [offer and reservation interface](../../system-model/contracts/interfaces/iface-003-one-loan-offer-reservation-and-loan-binding.md) say that publishing an offer is the lender's standing acceptance and that the reservation creates the loan without a second lender approval. The [one-loan offer selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-012-one-loan-offer-reservation-and-binding.md) says there is no second lender-approval step, the [excluded-scope record](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-011-public-multi-lender-pooling-custody-and-automatic-capital.md) also says no second lender approval, and the [origination flow](../../system-model/behavior/flows/flow-001-origination-preparation-and-qualification.md) states as a precondition that publication is standing acceptance. This domain's [funding vault state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-010-base-funding-vault.md) supports this side by listing a "second lender approval" among its invalid transitions. On the other side, the [offer and reservation state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-001-one-loan-offer-and-reservation.md), the [canonical terms state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-002-canonical-loan-terms.md), the origination flow's approval step and the terms responsibility require explicit borrower and lender approvals of the same final terms before commitment, and the offer and reservation state says the lender's final-terms approval is distinct from its earlier approval of the commercial offer. The terms responsibility also says publication approves the proposed commercial values only; that could reconcile the reservation wording with a later final-terms approval, but not the selection's statement that there is no second lender-approval step. The question is undecided. The domain is not affected in one respect: both sides keep funding as a separate, explicit capital action by the selected lender.

**Unclear: a paused deployment state.** The upgrade governance selection, the opening authority constraint and the contract responsibility say V1 has no on-chain pause. The [deployment and upgrade state](../../system-model/contracts/states/state-019-base-deployment-upgrade-and-opener.md) nevertheless lists pause and operational roles in a deployment's identity, and a "paused for new or risky action" state entered by an authorized operational role. The [opener and pause boundaries rule](../../system-model/contracts/invariants/inv-019-funding-opener-and-pause-boundaries.md) limits any such role. The records do not say whether that state is the operational frontend, API and opener stop or something on chain. Treat this as undecided. In either reading, the records forbid a pause or operational role from withdrawing funds, changing committed terms or recipients, or revealing secrets.

**Deferred:**

- Base accounting for cooperative settlement. The participants can authorize a cooperative Bitcoin settlement, but how it reconciles or closes the Base record is not designed, so the product may not claim it does; see [cooperative settlement operations](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-005-cooperative-settlement-operations.md).
- Making the main contract immutable, including how a new opener would continue afterwards. V1 keeps the upgrade authority and does not renounce it; see [immutable contract conversion](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-007-immutable-base-contract-conversion.md).
- Platform-paid submission of the acceptance and claim calls through a relayer or paymaster. V1 instead allows one bounded gas payment to the participant's own account for each call, never requires it, and the participant always signs and submits; see [platform-paid reveal execution](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-014-platform-paid-base-reveal-execution.md).
- Production gas funding for Vault payment accounts, which is a separate capability from that gas payment.

**Excluded:** public or multi-lender matching, pooled lender capital, product custody and automatic capital deployment ([excluded capital models](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-011-public-multi-lender-pooling-custody-and-automatic-capital.md)); Bitcoin liquidation, surplus return and shortfall enforcement ([liquidation exclusion](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-012-bitcoin-liquidation-surplus-and-shortfall.md)); and any legal conclusion, such as whether fixed recipients make the product legally non-custodial ([legal analysis exclusion](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-009-legal-and-regulatory-analysis.md)).

**What exists today.** The Base contract package is implemented and its local verification is accepted. Its contribution to the runnable local demonstration is incomplete. Base test-network and main-network deployment are not claimed, and the move from the beta key to the Safe-controlled timelock has not been performed; those are later deployment and operation gates. The Vault payment account's payment signing is planned and unverified. The [delivery status](../../build/workflow/handoffs/README.md) and the [runtime claims boundary](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-013-runtime-profile-claims-and-activation-gates.md) give the current position.

The [domain record](../../system-model/domains/base-usdc-settlement-and-funding/domain.md) lists exactly what the domain owns and excludes.
