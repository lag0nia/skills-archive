# Participant Access And Loan Operations

This domain turns the separate agreement, Vault, Base and Bitcoin authorities into a product that people can actually use: it decides who may act, records what the application durably accepted and what each external source reported, and runs an isolated local demonstration for developers. It coordinates every step of a loan, yet it holds no private key, gives no participant consent on anyone's behalf, controls no lender capital and never decides what happened on a chain. The [architecture guide](../architecture.md) shows where it fits in the whole system.

## What this domain does and who takes part

A loan involves several independent authorities. The loan terms say what was agreed. Each participant's **Vault**, a separate browser application on its own web origin, holds that participant's seed, keys and secrets under a password only the participant knows. The Base contracts decide funding, acceptance and repayment of USDC. Bitcoin consensus decides what happens to the collateral. None of these can, by itself, guide a borrower and a lender through a loan, survive a lost network response, or show each person where things stand. This domain supplies that coordination.

The people and systems it serves:

- **Participants**: a borrower and a lender, each signing in to the product web application and using their own Vault.
- **Workforce operators**: organization staff who sign in through a separate workforce boundary and can only request operations the design already fixes.
- **Workers and observers** from other domains: the Bitcoin operations worker, the Base observer and the fallback service, which exchange jobs, evidence and status with the application.
- **Developers**, who run the isolated local demonstration on their own machine.

A few ideas recur throughout this guide:

- **Account and role.** Every participant has one permanent internal account identity. A role (borrower or lender) is granted per loan. One account may be the borrower on one loan and the lender on another, but never both on the same loan.
- **Workspace.** The workspace is the durable application view of one loan, scoped to the reader's role. Each version of it carries a **revision** number, so a command can say exactly which state it was based on.
- **Command and operation.** A participant or operator changes something by sending a command. When the application accepts it, the command receives a stable **operation** identity that the sender can look up again later. The workspace is the current view; the operation is only the receipt and recovery record for one command.
- **Durable job and outbox.** Work that must happen after a change, such as asking a worker to broadcast a transaction, is recorded in the same database commit as the change itself. This record, the outbox, is what guarantees that a crash or a lost signal cannot lose the work.
- **Handoff.** A handoff lets one particular participant perform one bounded action in their Vault, such as signing an exact set of transactions. It is not a general signing token.
- **Payment account and payment intent.** A Vault can hold a separate Base account for paying USDC. Before the Vault signs any payment, the application records a payment intent that fixes one exact public request. This capability is selected, but its implementation and verification evidence are still outstanding (see below).

The domain keeps a strict vocabulary of evidence. A request the application accepted, a job it completed locally, a transaction it submitted, an event an observer saw, an event that is final on Base, a Bitcoin transaction broadcast and a Bitcoin transaction confirmed are seven different facts. The application never collapses them into one "done".

## How its responsibilities work together

[Participant identity and role access](../../system-model/domains/participant-access-and-loan-operations/responsibilities/sr-010-participant-identity-and-role-access.md) decides who someone is and what they may do. Participants sign in through WorkOS, an external authentication provider, with Google or a six-digit code sent to a verified email address. WorkOS is used only behind narrow adapters: the provider's subject identifier is merely a link to the permanent internal account, and the application's own database alone grants loan roles. Sessions last at most seven days and end after 24 hours of inactivity. Sensitive actions, such as retrieving encrypted Vault data, requesting seed display, exporting a current partially signed Bitcoin transaction (PSBT), opening a Vault handoff or replacing the Vault password, need a fresh reauthentication no older than 15 minutes; a participant who has enrolled a time-based one-time password (TOTP) must also pass that check, and replacing the Vault password additionally needs the current Vault password plus a single-use email confirmation that carries no Vault material. Signing in can authorize retrieval of a participant's own encrypted Vault data and packages. It can never decrypt the Vault, recover a forgotten Vault password, seed or secret, or make a chain action valid.

[Loan workspace and application coordination](../../system-model/domains/participant-access-and-loan-operations/responsibilities/sr-011-loan-workspace-and-application-coordination.md) is the application's working memory and its coordinator. Its application interface keeps the workspace, commands, operations and durable jobs in a Cloudflare D1 database. It holds offers and reservations as product records, issues handoffs, stores signed transaction packages, schedules fixed-effect automation, sends product notifications, shows each role its current status with the source of every fact, and gives workforce operators their narrow tools. It never acquires participant private authority, lender capital or chain truth.

[Local runtime composition and controlled demonstration](../../system-model/domains/participant-access-and-loan-operations/responsibilities/sr-012-local-runtime-composition-and-controlled-demonstration.md) assembles the independently built parts of the system on a developer machine, through the same public interfaces they use elsewhere, into one runnable local demonstration with two isolated participants. It owns only that composition and its lifecycle; every record inside it keeps its usual owner.

The three cooperate in a fixed pattern. Every request reaching the workspace is checked independently by the identity and role responsibility: being signed in, or reaching an internal method, is never treated as permission for a different action. The workspace then records the accepted command and its follow-up work together, and the Vaults, workers and observers act through their own contracts. The local demonstration drives this same pattern through real browsers.

**What it receives and provides.** From [Loan Terms And Commitments](loan-terms-and-commitments.md) it receives the offer, the reservation and the loan's terms, and it hands back authenticated participants and their approvals; it never redefines committed terms. From [Bitcoin Authorization And Recovery](bitcoin-authorization-and-recovery.md) it receives the Vaults' public identities and signed results and the Bitcoin operations worker's unsigned signing material and evidence; it provides handoffs, stored packages and durable jobs. From [Base USDC Settlement And Funding](base-usdc-settlement-and-funding.md), through [Base Observation And Fallback Review](base-observation-and-fallback-review.md), it receives contract events, and relies only on those that are final; it also shows the fallback secret's public status exactly as that service reports it. It provides each participant and operator with a sourced view of all of this.

## A normal case: one loan through the workspace

The following case is illustrative. A lender has published an offer; a borrower signs in and wants it.

```figure
caption: Each step leaves its own record; the workspace shows the step as done only when its own source says so.
step: Sign in | The borrower and lender authenticate; the database grants each a role on this loan.
step: Reserve | The workspace records the reservation, which creates the loan's identity.
step: Prepare in Vaults | Separate handoffs let each Vault allocate loan keys and confirm its destinations.
step: Approve terms | The workspace assembles one exact candidate; whether the lender approves it again is open.
step: Sign and archive | Each Vault signs an exact batch; the package is stored before a receipt exists.
step: Fund and accept | Only final Base events advance the loan; the workspace schedules activation.
step: Repay and recover | After the lender's final claim, the borrower's recovery is broadcast and confirmed.
```

**Sign in and reserve.** Both participants authenticate. When the borrower reserves the offer, the workspace records the reservation, which creates the loan's identity and its draft commercial terms. The terms domain owns what these mean.

**Prepare in the Vaults.** For each role, the application issues an allocation handoff. The product passes the Vault a short-lived, single-use random code in the address fragment of the link. The Vault establishes the same participant through its own fresh session, redeems the code atomically, removes it from the address bar, reloads the exact action from the application and checks it independently. The Vault then allocates this loan's keys, saves its encrypted state and returns only public results. The workspace accepts a result only after the encrypted Vault snapshot it names has been acknowledged as current, and shows each role's step as pending or completed, so a refresh or restart finds the same state. A second, separate handoff per role confirms that role's payout destinations and creates its loan secret only if none exists yet: a retry reuses the saved secret, and changed destinations require a new request but never a new secret. No package or signing handoff exists until both allocation results and committed terms exist.

**Approve the final terms.** The workspace assembles one complete candidate version of the terms with its hash and shows each role readable terms, both bound public identities and each role's approval state. This domain's identity and workspace records, like the terms state and responsibility records, have both the borrower and the lender approve that exact candidate; an approval names the candidate version and hash, a repeat of the identical approval changes nothing, any change to a hashed field voids earlier approvals, and the second matching approval commits the terms once while the reservation is still active. The architecture record and the offer records instead say that publishing the offer is the lender's standing acceptance and that the reservation needs no second lender approval, and the offer selection adds that there is no second lender-approval step. Whether these describe different stages or contradict each other is undecided. Either way, an approval grants no Vault custody, signing, funding or chain authority.

**Sign and archive the package.** Once the borrower's Bitcoin deposit is observed, the Bitcoin operations worker gives the application every non-secret fact needed for signing. The application validates and stores one immutable, unsigned signing batch, and the product launches a fresh handoff bound to the acknowledged Vault revision. Each Vault signs locally and returns the exact signed batch. The application checks that every required signature is present and valid, writes the complete package directly to content-addressed storage, and only then records its receipt in the database. That order (unsigned batch, handoff, Vault signing, verification, storage, receipt) never changes: a receipt can only exist after signing, so the ability to sign depends on a current unsigned batch, never on a receipt. The receipt for the initial package is required before the loan can qualify for funding, which also needs six unchanged confirmations of the deposit, fresh agreement between two Bitcoin sources and at least 67 hours before the deposit's refund could become valid; an unconfirmed deposit opens no funding window. the receipt for the later recovery package is what lets the loan operate with that package ready.

**Fund, accept and activate.** The lender funds the loan's funding vault on Base from any wallet it controls. A transfer, a balance or a submitted transaction is only provisional; the workspace moves on only when the Base observer reports the funding as final. The borrower then deliberately accepts in the Vault, only while funding is no older than 24 hours and at least 19 hours remain before the deposit's refund could become valid, which discloses the borrower's secret X through the Base contract. When that acceptance is final, the workspace records activation as pending and schedules the Bitcoin operations worker to complete and broadcast the pre-signed activation with X. Only independent Bitcoin evidence, not the worker's completion, establishes that activation confirmed.

**Repay and recover.** Repayment follows the same rule: the borrower's transfer is provisional until the repayment is final on Base. The lender then claims, after a fresh reauthentication, by revealing Y in the Vault strictly before the claim cutoff C, two days after the deadline. If the lender misses C, the claim is gone for good and the workspace schedules the fixed refund of the repayment to the borrower at C (see the [Base Observation And Fallback Review guide](base-observation-and-fallback-review.md)). Once a claim is final, the workspace automatically schedules the borrower's pre-signed recovery transaction, and the loan closes only when independent Bitcoin sources agree that the spend has confirmed.

What proves the result at each point is the external source, not the workspace. The authoritative step sequences are in the [origination flow](../../system-model/behavior/flows/flow-001-origination-preparation-and-qualification.md), the [activation flow](../../system-model/behavior/flows/flow-002-activation-completion-and-active-entry.md) and the [normal repayment flow](../../system-model/behavior/flows/flow-004-normal-repayment-close.md).

## How commands survive lost responses and restarts

A browser may lose a response, a tab may be closed, or a worker may crash. The workspace is designed so that none of these creates a second action or a false outcome.

- **Idempotent commands.** Each command carries an idempotency key and the revision it was based on. The same key with the same request body always resolves to the same operation; the same key with a different body is rejected without any change. A command based on a stale revision is rejected, and the sender refreshes the workspace first.
- **Atomic acceptance.** An operation identity is returned only after the request fingerprint, any local change, the operation record and any required job have committed together. The original authorized caller can retrieve the operation after a timeout, refresh, retry, process restart or browser restart, and a completed operation links to the resulting workspace revision.
- **Honest reads.** A participant can tell an absent loan, a forbidden action, a stale view and an unavailable dependency apart. An unavailable dependency is never shown as a current cached success. For denials, invalid or expired authentication is answered as unauthenticated; a resource that does not exist and one outside the participant's own account or loan look identical ("not found"), so the application never reveals that someone else's loan exists; "forbidden" is reserved for a resource the participant may know about but whose requested action the role does not allow.
- **Jobs that cannot be lost.** After a job commits, the application may send an immediate wake to the worker. That wake is only an optimization: it carries no authority, and if it is lost or duplicated, a scan of due jobs every minute finds the work anyway. Workers hold renewable two-minute leases. Failed work is retried after 1, 2, 5 and 15 minutes and then hourly, but each retry first reloads the database and the canonical sources to prove that the identical action is still required.
- **Ambiguous outcomes.** If a submission times out, the exact request, route, participant, amount, nonce, fee, signed bytes, provider request and transaction identities are kept, and the operation enters reconciliation instead of being resubmitted. A retry can never change the caller, recipient, amount, route, package, nonce, gas, fee cap or signed bytes. Ambiguous financial results, provider conflicts and invariant failures alert immediately; ordinary unresolved retries warn after 15 minutes and become critical after an hour; safety-critical reconciliation continues until the action completes, is shown obsolete or is permanently rejected, and demands operator attention after 15 minutes. Nobody resolves it by overriding a chain fact by hand.

A mock, a health check, an internal service method or a test inside one component does not prove that a real consumer can do any of this. Only the public interface, exercised by the actual product client, counts.

## Paying from the Vault

> **Status: selected; implementation and verification evidence outstanding**
>
> The Vault payment account is a selected design. Its selection records delivery status as partial: implementation and execution evidence are still outstanding, the Vault's payment signing format has no known-answer test vectors yet and is not verified, and the delivery records claim no current payment implementation receipt. A bounded correction to the application interface and the product's transfer review passes focused checks, but it is not evidence for the payment account, and its delivery snapshots and completion receipts are not finalized. Everything in this section describes the target design.

Funding and repayment on Base are payer-neutral: the contracts accept the exact USDC from any address. The selected design gives each participant a dedicated payment account inside their Vault, so they can pay without an external browser wallet.

**A separate, recoverable account.** The payment account is generated with its own random key, one per participant and supported Base network. It is distinct from the participant's Base protocol account (the account that makes the loan's own contract calls), from every per-loan Bitcoin key and from the secrets X and Y. Its address is registered and shown for deposits only after the Vault's current encrypted snapshot and the participant's required backup acknowledgement prove that the same key, and therefore the same address, can be restored. An address shown before that point could receive funds nobody can recover. Losing the Vault password still loses the key: no server, support, product account, seed phrase or old backup reconstructs it, and creating a new account is an explicit new identity that does not move funds at the old address.

**One exact intent before signing.** Each transfer has one purpose: funding, repayment, or a narrow participant transfer. The application records a payment intent that fixes the account, network, USDC contract, recipient, amount, zero native value, one reserved nonce (the account's transaction sequence number) and the full gas and fee envelope. The participant approves exactly those fields in the Vault; changing any of them voids the approval and needs a new intent. Responsibility is split: the application interface owns the public intent, the account's nonce lease and a fencing generation that stale tabs, devices or restores cannot pass, while the Vault alone holds the encrypted pending transaction and signs.

```figure
caption: Each phase is recorded before the next begins, so a crash can always be resolved from what was written.
step: Recorded | The application stores the exact public intent.
step: Locked and approved | The application holds the nonce lease; the participant approves the exact transfer and fees.
step: Signing | The application durably enters this phase before the Vault signer is called.
step: Hash persisted | The Vault saves the signed transaction encrypted and backed up; the application stores its public hash.
step: Broadcast attempted | A marker is written, then the transaction is sent to the network.
step: Observed | Inclusion, success or revert is observed; loan state still waits for the final contract event.
```

Two consequences matter most. Before **Signing**, the intent can be cancelled or shown never submitted, and the nonce is released. Once **Signing** exists, a timeout is not proof that nothing was sent: recovery must find the exact pending transaction, either from the current or restored encrypted Vault record or by verified deterministic reproduction of the same request. A repeat broadcast may only resend the identical signed bytes; new signing, a fee bump, a replacement or cancellation after **Signing** is outside this version.

**Bounded reconciliation.** When an outcome is ambiguous, the application probes the exact transaction at 0, 15 and 60 seconds and escalates at 180 seconds. A known pending transaction whose inclusion is simply delayed is **Accepted pending**, which is normal and not a failure. After escalation a participant or authorized operator may explicitly probe again or resend the identical bytes at the same nonce and fees. When the bounded route cannot establish a safe outcome, for example because neither the pending record nor a verified reproduction is available or the evidence contradicts itself, the intent moves to **Reconciliation blocked** and the account becomes visibly **Blocked** rather than reconciling forever. It stays blocked until explicit recovery or decision, such as restoring the current Vault snapshot and passing the same-account checks. In the public profile the design calls for two independently operated Base providers, a requirement that is deferred; the local demonstration uses one local node plus the independent Base observer and makes no multi-provider claim.

**Nonces and reorganizations.** A success or revert releases the intent's signing lock, but its nonce stays occupied until finality, so later transfers use only a proven-safe higher nonce. If a Base reorganization removes an observed transaction, later transfers wait until the ledger is reconciled in order; a finality violation blocks the account for incident handling rather than paying twice or silently reversing loan state.

**The narrow participant transfer.** A participant can send USDC that arrived at their payment account onward: only the configured USDC token, to a validated recipient they choose, a positive amount, zero native value, with sufficient USDC and native gas and a fresh exact approval. It has no loan or workspace binding, and its confirmation updates only its own transfer record; it can never advance or change a loan. Arbitrary contract calls, token approvals, swaps, other assets, native-value sends and generic signing are excluded. A payment address may become a principal, settlement or refund recipient only for a new loan, through the existing terms fields, with role authorization, fresh approvals and disclosure of this separate transfer capability; existing loans keep their committed addresses.

**What it does not change.** None of this alters historical terms, the payer-neutral contracts or observation authority. Only the final Base contract events advance funding or repayment; a transaction hash, a balance or an observed transfer does not.

## The local demonstration

The required outcome of this design is a runnable local demonstration, not a deployment. A developer runs one documented command to start or resume the whole system, with paired commands to read status and logs, to shut down while keeping state, and to reset and reseed explicitly.

**What it runs.** Everything binds to the local machine only. A local Base-compatible chain (Anvil, at chain 31337) carries the loan contracts and a fake USDC token. Two connected, persistent Bitcoin nodes run on regtest, Bitcoin's local test network, with named demo wallets and controlled mining. Two demo participants, a borrower and a lender, have separate browser sessions and real, separately encrypted Vaults, with conspicuously local-only authentication. The real product web application, Vault, application interface, Base observer and Bitcoin operations worker run through their public interfaces. The demo first has both Vaults register their public protocol accounts, then funds those accounts with local gas without ever holding their keys, and only then seeds the offers and loans bound to them; the chain's built-in test accounts act only as external funders and never become protocol accounts. Under the payment design above, the demo also establishes each participant's recoverable payment account first and gives its public address disposable local ether and USDC. That setup funding is never evidence of a participant payment and is not gas sponsorship.

**Preserved state and explicit reset.** A small coordination record in the product repository references the state each producer owns, such as the local chain and the Bitcoin wallets, by path and fingerprint; it does not own that state. Ordinary shutdown stops only the demo's own processes, in reverse dependency order, and keeps everything. A compatible restart resumes without silently redeploying or reseeding. Only the explicit reset validates the coordination record, stops owned processes, asks each producer to reset its own bounded state, and reseeds a clean scenario. It never recursively deletes a sibling repository or a producer's own directory.

**Failing closed.** The demo refuses to start, before any side effect, if anything points at a public network or credential, if the chain or Bitcoin topology is wrong, if a component's version or state fingerprint does not match, or if a port collides. A failure names the owning component and the last successful stage, keeps diagnostic state and never triggers a reset. Its ready state proves only that the local health checks pass; it never establishes a loan step.

**What it proves.** A browser-driven acceptance run takes the two participants through Vault setup, backup and restore, a real product-to-Vault handoff, origination, activation, normal repayment and confirmed borrower recovery, and records a redacted receipt. Default, the fallback service, cooperative settlement, fee-bump children and public networks are outside that journey. The human-facing acceptance additionally must prove wallet setup with an installed browser-extension wallet, according to the local-demo constraint and acceptance selection; the payment-account selection, by contrast, calls the external-wallet path historical regression evidence that is not the current participant journey, and the payment journey must use the Vault. Whether the extension-wallet proof is still a separate current requirement is undecided.

**Current status.** An earlier automated run of the full journey passed in three browser engines using the historical injected-wallet path; it remains regression evidence only and does not prove the current journey. The current local-demonstration delivery is blocked: it waits for the actual application-interface changes for terms preparation, the Vault and product consumers of those changes, and new cross-domain verification evidence. Its supervisor still contains an explicit not-implemented stop, and its required verification checks have not passed.

## Exceptions and recovery

- **Lost response, refresh or restart.** The participant reloads the workspace and looks up the operation; the same request returns the same result, and nothing is resubmitted blindly.
- **Wrong or stale request.** A stale revision, altered idempotent request, wrong participant, loan, chain, contract, action, account, recipient, amount, nonce, fee, package or secret binding, or an expired, replayed or already redeemed handoff code is rejected without change. A fresh handoff code is issued only for an action that is still current and unchanged.
- **Product account recovery.** Recovering product access restores sign-in and retrieval of the participant's encrypted Vault data. It never decrypts or changes the Vault without the current Vault password; a forgotten Vault password cannot be recovered.
- **Backup trouble.** Signed packages and Vault ciphertext are copied hourly, and the database is exported daily, to separately locked storage outside Cloudflare. Backup delay raises a warning at two hours and a critical alert at six; at 24 hours new loans stop qualifying, but existing loans are not invalidated. The design accepts a bounded window in which a newly written package exists only in Cloudflare until the next successful backup.
- **Missing signatures after activation.** If recovery transactions remain unsigned after the activation confirms, the application alerts and keeps prompting; it does not present the loan as package-ready.
- **Notifications.** Product email is retried for at most 24 hours and then shown as failed. It never contains a secret or an action link, never changes loan state and never proves that a participant saw it.
- **A payment account that cannot be resolved.** An account whose key is unavailable shows as unavailable; one whose intent cannot be safely classified becomes visibly blocked. Neither silently generates a new address.
- **Cloudflare outage.** Every production part shares Cloudflare's provider failure domain despite separate origins, projects, credentials and releases, so a Cloudflare-wide outage can suspend the product, Vault, application interface and database, observers and automation together. The chains keep running; the application accepts no new consequential command and claims no chain result. External monitoring outside Cloudflare can still report the outage. After recovery, the application reconciles Base and Bitcoin before resuming its idempotent jobs. The off-provider backups protect data, not service availability, and no recovery time or data-loss limit is promised.
- **Contested facts.** A source conflict or stale evidence stops only the automation that depends on the contested fact, and unknown results are quarantined rather than guessed.

## Authority boundaries

**What this domain decides.** Who a participant or operator is and which role they hold; whether a request is authorized and accepted; which durable jobs exist and when they run; what each role sees, with the source of every fact; when to issue a handoff; when a signed package is stored and receipted; and, for the local demonstration, its own lifecycle.

**What it must not decide or imply.** It never holds or recovers Vault passwords, seeds, private keys or unrevealed X or Y, and never signs a Bitcoin transaction or a participant's Base call. It does not choose destinations, define terms, move lender capital, judge the fallback evidence or broadcast a lender default automatically. Signing in, a role, account recovery, multi-factor authentication (MFA), a reservation, an allocation, a prompt, a notification, a balance, a submission, a worker's completion or silence from an observer never implies final-terms approval, chain finality, Bitcoin confirmation, default, a beneficiary choice or secret recovery. For gas sponsorship it only requests and tracks one bounded stipend sent to the participant's protocol account; the platform never builds, signs or submits a participant call that carries a secret.

**Workforce operators.** Staff reach a dedicated operator hostname protected by Cloudflare Access, using an approved company Google identity, a mandatory independent TOTP code and a session of at most 15 minutes; participant sign-in grants no workforce authority. Every request also needs an active internal operator identity with the exact permission. A viewer only reads. An operator may perform only fixed actions, such as acknowledging incidents, pausing the application's own automation, requesting rechecks, choosing an already-signed fee tier, exporting one exact reviewed fee-bump request, or requesting Base calls whose effect is already fixed. A security administrator manages operator membership. One authorized operator suffices for a manual action, and there is no break-glass path. Every privileged attempt, including rejected ones, is recorded in an audit operators cannot edit and kept for seven years; the audit shows who asked, never that a chain result happened.

**The local demonstration.** It owns only its isolated profile. Demo identities, tokens, wallets, funds, contracts, fixtures and reset actions are unreachable or rejected outside that profile, and a passing demonstration proves nothing about testnet, mainnet, production operation, a security audit, paid providers or production funding.

## Choices and their reasons

- **WorkOS behind narrow adapters.** Using WorkOS with Google and email codes, the session limits and database-owned roles was approved on the agent's recommendation; the user specified that TOTP is optional per participant and that each runtime isolates WorkOS in a replaceable adapter. The recorded aim is product sign-in that stays separate from the Vault password and can be replaced without rewriting participant identity.
- **One Cloudflare failure domain.** The user specified Cloudflare for the production application; the outage behavior was approved on the agent's recommendation. The recorded reason is simplicity: spreading parts across providers would multiply identity, replication, deployment and incident paths before those operations are proven, so the design accepts that a Cloudflare-wide outage stops the application and Bitcoin automation together.
- **A consumer-visible workspace and operation.** The user specified one bounded role-scoped workspace view and a retrievable operation for every accepted command; some details were the agent's choice. The recorded reason is that an internal service method or a response inside one component did not let a browser acquire current state or recover a durable result after a lost response or restart.
- **The database as the only job authority.** Atomic state-and-job commits, the immediate wake with a one-minute scan, leases, retry timing and alert thresholds were approved on the agent's recommendation.
- **Non-revealing denials and the workforce boundary.** The denial rules were approved on the agent's recommendation. For workforce access, the user specified company Google identities with a TOTP code; the rest of the boundary was approved on the agent's recommendation.
- **A separately owned local demonstration.** Separating the runtime-profile claims and giving the demonstration one owned entrypoint, preserved state and explicit reset were approved on the agent's recommendation; supervisor and port details were the agent's choice. The recorded reasons are that existing root scripts had no owner for their lifecycle and deleted their state on shutdown, that a root command without assembly ownership could not close runnable use, and that implemented parts plus a deterministic harness had been treated as broad completion.
- **A Vault-owned payment account.** The recorded reasons are that an external wallet leaves payment authority outside the Vault and needs a browser extension, that deriving payments from the protocol account would collapse the caller, gas and payment boundaries, that an address must not be exposed before its key is recoverable, and that the exact request must be durable before signing so lost responses and concurrent use can be reconciled. The records cannot establish who chose these payment-account decisions: their origin is recorded as unknown.

## Open, excluded and deferred

**Open questions.** Three points are recorded as undecided; this guide does not settle them.

- *Lender approval of the final terms.* This domain's identity and workspace records, with the terms state and responsibility records, have both participants approve the final candidate before commitment; the architecture record and the offer records say the published offer is the lender's standing acceptance and the reservation needs no second lender approval. Whether these are different stages or a contradiction is undecided. See the [Loan Terms And Commitments guide](loan-terms-and-commitments.md).
- *Expiry of an unused reservation.* The reservation state record says a reservation that reaches its 24-hour expiry before the terms are committed, with no prefund attempted, simply expires; the infraction rule applies only after commitment. The offer selection and the reservation contract apply cancellation, reopening, a block on that borrower and one infraction to any missing prefund at the deadline. Whether the uncommitted case incurs the infraction is undecided.
- *The extension wallet in local acceptance.* The local-demo constraint and acceptance selection require human-facing acceptance to prove setup of an installed browser-extension wallet; the payment-account selection treats the external-wallet path as historical and not the current journey. Whether that proof remains a current requirement is undecided.

**What is and is not claimed.**

| Claim | Status now |
| --- | --- |
| Deterministic local verification | Required, verification only. |
| Runnable local demonstration | Required; current delivery is blocked. |
| Testnet deployment | Not claimed; a later deployment gate. |
| Mainnet deployment | Not claimed; a later deployment gate. |
| Activated production operation | Not claimed; later activation and operation gates. |

A configuration name, a healthy process, a seeded fixture, a passing test or a local demonstration receipt cannot satisfy any later gate, and a testnet deployment would not establish mainnet or production operation.

**Excluded or deferred capabilities.** Each of these is recorded as out of scope for this version, with a condition for revisiting it.

- *Forgotten Vault-password recovery.* Deferred; the user approved having no forgotten-password recovery in this version on the agent's recommendation. A stronger mechanism needs its own threat, custody and migration design. See [stronger participant-Vault recovery](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-006-stronger-participant-vault-recovery.md).
- *Cooperative settlement operations.* The two-party Bitcoin mechanism is fixed, but the signing session, Base accounting and support process are deferred (the agent's choice), so the product cannot claim cooperative settlement is operational. See [cooperative-settlement operations](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-005-cooperative-settlement-operations.md).
- *Replacing WorkOS.* Building an application-owned authentication service is deferred, as the user specified. A replacement would have to keep internal account, loan and Vault identities and prove authenticated linking, session, MFA and recovery parity, cutover, rollback and adapter conformance; Better Auth is a recorded future candidate, not a dependency. See [authentication replacement](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-015-application-owned-authentication-replacement.md).
- *Platform-paid reveal transactions.* A relayer or paymaster that would pay for the borrower's acceptance or the lender's claim on Base is deferred. Today the only support is a bounded gas stipend to the participant's protocol account, with the participant signing and submitting directly. See [platform-paid reveal execution](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-014-platform-paid-base-reveal-execution.md).
- *Production gas for payment accounts.* Deferred; the protocol-account stipend never refills a payment account, and local demo funding is setup only. See the [payment-gas ticket](../../build/workflow/tickets/base-usdc-settlement-and-funding/sr-005/sr-005-tickets.yaml).
- *Automatic lender-default broadcast.* Outside this version: the organization does not schedule, finalize or broadcast a lender-default transaction, and a lender may export the current valid package for its own broadcast only after its Bitcoin condition is valid. See [automatic lender-default broadcast](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-001-automatic-lender-default-broadcast.md).

**Later gates.** The public rule requiring two independent Base providers for payment reconciliation is deferred, and the beta deliverability of Cloudflare Email Sending remains a release gate.

The [domain record](../../system-model/domains/participant-access-and-loan-operations/domain.md) lists exactly what the domain owns and excludes, and the [runtime profile claims record](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-013-runtime-profile-claims-and-activation-gates.md) holds the exact evidence each claim needs.
