# Base USDC Settlement And Funding

This domain owns everything that happens to USDC on Base for one Bitcoin-backed loan: the lender funding it, the borrower accepting it and receiving the principal, repayment, the lender's claim and every refund. Contracts enforce fixed amounts, deadlines and recipients, so no payer, caller or worker can choose where the money goes, and each Base step stays separate from the Bitcoin collateral it relates to. The [architecture guide](../architecture.md) shows where this domain fits in the whole system.

## What this domain does

Each loan spans two chains with separate authorities. The Bitcoin collateral sits in an escrow governed by Bitcoin consensus and by transactions the participants signed in advance, as the [Bitcoin Authorization And Recovery guide](bitcoin-authorization-and-recovery.md) explains. The USDC side lives on Base, a network that settles to Ethereum, where one loan contract keeps an authoritative record for each loan and moves USDC only by fixed rules. This domain owns that USDC side and nothing on the Bitcoin side. A Base payout can make public a secret that completes a Bitcoin transaction the participants already signed, but nothing on Base signs, broadcasts or confirms a Bitcoin transaction.

The lender's principal and the borrower's repayment both pass through these contracts. If anyone could choose where that USDC goes, the system would hold its users' money in practice; if a balance could be mistaken for repayment, collateral could be released on a false fact. The domain's contracts are built so that no ordinary call can do either; the trust that remains in contract upgrades is explained under authority boundaries.

A few concepts are needed throughout:

- **The main loan contract and its loan record.** One upgradeable contract on Base holds one record per loan: the hash of the committed terms, the fixed Base recipients, the borrower's committed protocol address, principal and debt, the hashes of the two participants' secrets, the deadlines and the lifecycle state. It never stores who sent money, a plaintext secret, Bitcoin data, a key or anything from a participant's Vault.
- **The funding vault.** A minimal contract deployed for one loan when its funding window opens, at an address that receives the lender's principal. It is fixed at deployment to the loan, native USDC on Base, the exact principal, the funding deadline, the main contract and the lender's settlement recipient, and it has no owner, administrator, upgrade path, arbitrary call or general rescue function.
- **The repayment vault.** A second minimal contract for the same loan, created at the moment the borrower accepts, that receives repayment. It is fixed to the loan, native USDC, the exact debt, the repayment deadline and the main contract, with the same absence of owners and rescue functions. It does not store a refund recipient; the main contract holds that.
- **Provisional versus final.** A transfer into a vault, a vault balance, a submitted transaction or a worker's report is provisional. Funding or repayment happens only when a named finalization call on the main contract moves the exact required amount, and the rest of the system relies on that call's event only after its Base block is final on Ethereum, the layer-1 chain Base settles to (Base L1 finality, roughly 20 minutes). The [Base Observation And Fallback Review guide](base-observation-and-fallback-review.md) explains how that finality is established.
- **Payer-neutral.** Anyone may send USDC to a vault. Sending gives no borrower or lender role, no choice of recipient and no refund right. Recipients come from the approved loan and the contract's state, never from who paid.
- **Fixed recipients.** The terms fix the borrower's Base principal recipient and the lender's Base settlement recipient, which also receives every funding refund. A third recipient, the borrower's repayment-refund recipient, receives any repayment USDC that goes back to the borrower; it starts as the borrower's principal recipient and can change at most once, as described below.
- **Permissionless fixed-effect calls.** Most contract calls can be submitted by anyone. Submitting one lets the caller pay the network fee for it; it gives no control over the amount, the recipient or what the call means.
- **The secrets `X` and `Y`.** Each participant's Vault, the dedicated browser application that holds that participant's keys and secrets, keeps one secret per loan. The borrower's `X`, revealed on Base, accepts the loan and becomes the value that lets the Bitcoin activation transaction be completed. The lender's `Y`, revealed on Base, claims the repaid USDC and becomes the value that completes the borrower's already-signed Bitcoin recovery transaction. The contract stores only their hashes.
- **The protocol address.** Each participant's Vault derives a Base account, committed into the loan terms, that signs that participant's acceptance or claim and, for the borrower, the refund-recipient switch. It is separate from the recipients and from any wallet that pays.
- **The deadlines.** `D` is the repayment deadline, a fixed timestamp in the committed terms. `C`, the claim cutoff, is `D` plus 172,800 seconds (48 hours). `T`, when the lender's pre-signed Bitcoin default transaction becomes valid, is `D` plus 259,200 seconds (72 hours); Bitcoin measures it by its own median time past, not by Base time.

## How its responsibilities work together

[Base loan state and USDC settlement](../../system-model/domains/base-usdc-settlement-and-funding/responsibilities/sr-003-base-loan-state-and-usdc-settlement.md) is the contract side. It owns the loan record, deploys and registers both vaults, moves exact amounts, enforces the deadlines and fixed recipients, and makes the lender's claim and the borrower's refund mutually exclusive. Every successful call emits one named event; every invalid call is rejected with a named error, moves nothing and emits nothing. It also owns the contract's upgrade and opener boundary. It never treats funding, acceptance, Bitcoin activation, repayment, claim, refund or default as interchangeable.

[Lender-controlled funding-vault interaction](../../system-model/domains/base-usdc-settlement-and-funding/responsibilities/sr-005-lender-controlled-funding-vault-interaction.md) is the lender's side of funding. Once the funding window's opening event is final, it presents the deployed funding vault and the facts the lender must verify: the chain, the contract deployment, the asset, the loan, the principal still missing, the deadline and the lender's fixed settlement recipient. The lender alone decides whether and when to transfer, and from which wallet or treasury it controls; one or several ordinary transfers are fine, and partial deposits add up. The responsibility shows the provisional balance and coordinates the permissionless finalization and any recovery, but it never signs for the lender, holds or pools capital, substitutes another lender, or draws funds automatically. Only the main contract's final events advance the loan.

The two cooperate in one direction: the funding interaction brings the lender's own transfer into the vault, and the contract turns it into funding only through exact finalization. The same contract then pays the borrower, receives repayment and settles it.

What the domain receives:

- from the [Loan Terms And Commitments guide](loan-terms-and-commitments.md): the committed terms and their hash, the fixed recipients, the committed protocol addresses, the deployment identity, and the one selected lender;
- from the Bitcoin domain, through each participant's Vault: the locally prepared and signed acceptance and claim calls, and the borrower's one-time refund-recipient switch;
- from the [Participant Access And Loan Operations guide](participant-access-and-loan-operations.md): the application's coordination, namely the request to open a funding window once Bitcoin qualification passes, a worker that normally submits finalizations, refunds and sweeps, the guided funding and repayment screens, and the records of payments made from Vault payment accounts.

What it provides:

- named events, errors, loan state and fixed value movements, which the observation domain classifies for finality and the application workspace turns into what participants see;
- the public `X` and `Y`, through the acceptance and claim events, which Bitcoin finalization may use only after those events are final;
- the `UnclaimedRepaymentRefunded` event, the only positive Base trigger for the conditional Bitcoin fallback described in the [Base Observation And Fallback Review guide](base-observation-and-fallback-review.md).

## A normal case

Suppose a lender's one-loan offer has been reserved by a borrower, the loan's terms are committed, and the borrower's Bitcoin prefund has qualified (the Bitcoin domain covers that part). Say, for illustration only, that the principal is 10,000 USDC and the debt 10,300 USDC.

```figure
caption: Each step counts only when its event is final on Base; Bitcoin activation and recovery happen separately, in the Bitcoin domain.
step: Window opens | The dedicated opener creates the loan record and its funding vault; a 24-hour funding window starts.
step: Lender transfers | The selected lender sends USDC to the vault from any wallet it controls, in one or more transfers.
step: Funding finalized | Anyone triggers finalization; exactly the principal moves into the main contract and a 24-hour acceptance lifetime starts.
step: Borrower accepts | Revealing X creates the repayment vault, pays the principal to the borrower's fixed recipient and makes X public, all at once.
step: Repayment finalized | No later than D, anyone triggers finalization; exactly the debt moves into the main contract.
step: Lender claims | Strictly before C, revealing Y pays the debt once to the lender's fixed recipient and makes Y public.
```

**Opening.** Only one dedicated, platform-funded opener account may open a funding window, and the application lets it do so only for a committed loan whose signed activation and refund transactions are durably recorded and whose Bitcoin prefund has six unchanged confirmations, with fresh agreement between observers, while at least 67 hours remain before the borrower's prefund refund could become valid. The contract recomputes the terms hash and rejects an existing loan identifier, but it cannot see Bitcoin, so it trusts the opener for that qualification alone. Opening creates the record and deploys the funding vault before its address is ever shown, and the 24-hour window starts when the opening transaction is included.

**Funding.** The lender checks the presented facts and transfers, say, 6,000 USDC and later 4,000 USDC. The product shows the provisional balance and the amount still missing. Once the balance reaches the principal before the deadline, the application's worker normally submits the finalization call from a fee wallet that has no role in the contract; the lender or anyone else may submit the same call first, and that counts as success. Exactly 10,000 USDC moves into the main contract; anything above it stays in the vault for later return to the lender's settlement recipient. What proves funding is the `FundingFinalized` event once it is final, not the balance and not the worker's report.

**Acceptance.** The borrower then has 24 hours to accept. Before the borrower's Vault may even prepare the call, a gate over the Bitcoin package state must pass, including funding no older than 24 hours and at least 19 hours left before the prefund refund. The borrower first confirms a review of the non-secret facts; the Vault then unlocks `X`, builds the exact acceptance transaction, and asks for a second confirmation bound to those exact bytes before signing it with the borrower's protocol address and submitting it. The application never receives `X`. The platform may send that protocol address a bounded amount of ether (ETH) once to pay the network fee, and the borrower may always pay the fee itself. In one transaction the contract checks `X` against its hash, deploys and registers the repayment vault, pays exactly 10,000 USDC to the borrower's fixed principal recipient, records acceptance and makes `X` public; if any part fails, all of it reverts. The `XAccepted` event, once final, proves acceptance. It does not prove Bitcoin activation: the escrow is active only when separate Bitcoin evidence confirms the activation transaction and the terminal transactions for the escrow are fully signed and durably recorded, and only that evidence can make the loan eligible to be shown as active.

**Repayment.** Before showing the repayment vault, the product shows the borrower's current refund recipient and asks the borrower to confirm it or make the one-time switch. Anyone may then pay, the borrower or a third party, by ordinary transfer; no allowance or contract call is needed. The guided transfer closes one hour before `D` and warns that a later direct transfer may not be finalized in time. No later than `D`, a finalization call, normally from the worker, moves exactly 10,300 USDC into the main contract and emits `Repaid`; once final, that is valid repayment. A balance of 10,300 USDC before `D` is necessary but is not itself repayment.

**Claim.** The lender, after fresh sign-in with multi-factor authentication, prepares the claim in its Vault with the same two confirmations. The contract accepts the claim from any caller holding the matching `Y`, strictly before `C`; in the product the bound lender is the one who reveals it. The debt goes once to the lender's settlement recipient and `Y` becomes public. When the `YReleased` event is final, the observation domain hands `Y` to Bitcoin finalization, which completes the borrower's pre-signed recovery transaction; the Bitcoin recovery is complete only when independent Bitcoin sources agree that the spend has confirmed.

## Exceptions and recovery

### Funding or acceptance does not complete

If the funding deadline passes without finalization, anyone may close the attempt and send the vault's USDC to the lender's settlement recipient. A finalization that was submitted but not finalized before the deadline does not win. If the loan was funded but the borrower does not accept within the 24-hour lifetime, anyone may return exactly the principal once to the lender's settlement recipient. The borrower's Bitcoin prefund has its own refund route in the Bitcoin domain; the two recoveries are independent of each other. Once `X` is final, this unwinding is no longer possible: the Bitcoin domain must continue the selected activation instead.

### Too little, too much, too late, or the wrong asset

Partial transfers accumulate but are neither funding nor repayment. USDC that remains or arrives later can always be swept by anyone, and the sweep's destination is fixed:

- from the funding vault, after finalization or after expiry, only to the lender's settlement recipient;
- from the repayment vault, after finalization or strictly after `D`, only to the borrower's current refund recipient before `C` and to the frozen recipient from `C` on. Waiting until after `D` keeps a sweep from racing a finalization that is still valid.

After `D`, the debt can no longer be finalized. USDC that arrives late can be swept only to the borrower's refund recipient and does not count as repayment; what that means for the collateral is described in the Bitcoin domain. Tokens other than the configured USDC, or transfers on the wrong network, have no recovery promise and may remain stuck.

### The lender does not claim before C

At `C` the lender's claim becomes permanently impossible and the borrower's refund recipient freezes. From then on anyone may trigger a refund that returns exactly the repaid debt, once, to the frozen recipient; the right never expires. The application schedules this refund after the final `Repaid` and may pay its fee from the fee wallet, which gains no role; a refund submitted first by an outsider counts as success. Claim and refund can never both succeed.

Before `C`, whether before or after repayment or a claim, only the borrower's committed protocol address may switch the refund recipient, once and irreversibly, from the original recipient to itself. No one can choose an arbitrary address.

The final `UnclaimedRepaymentRefunded` event is the only positive Base fact that can trigger the conditional fallback, under which an independent evidence-conditioned validation service publishes a per-loan secret that completes the borrower's recovery transaction. `Repaid` alone, elapsed time, a missing `Y`, lender silence or a raw token transfer never is that trigger. The Base refund never waits for or depends on the fallback, and the fallback is not yet activated for any loan; the [Base Observation And Fallback Review guide](base-observation-and-fallback-review.md) explains its gate. A Base refund does not mean the borrower has recovered the Bitcoin: the collateral stays in escrow until a Bitcoin route confirms, and the lender's timelocked default route becomes valid after `T` independently of anything on Base. Lender silence after valid repayment is not borrower default.

### Base pays the principal but Bitcoin activation does not confirm

Base payment and Bitcoin activation are not one atomic transaction. After acceptance the borrower holds the principal; if the activation transaction fails to confirm before the borrower's prefund refund matures and that refund confirms first, the lender is left with unsecured credit. The gate before acceptance, the 19-hour reserve, durable activation-pending recovery and persistent rebroadcast make this harder but cannot eliminate it. The [record of this residual risk](../../system-model/contracts/security/sec-023-base-pays-principal-but-bitcoin-activation-does-not-confirm.md) describes it in full.

### Failed, ambiguous or repeated calls

A call with the wrong loan, hash, caller, recipient, amount, deadline, state or deployment reverts with a named error; it emits no event and moves no money. A reverted, simulated, submitted or locally completed call is not a transition. When an outcome is unknown, the application reconciles the exact transaction and event before retrying, and a retry can only repeat the same fixed effect. A payment from a Vault payment account is recorded as an exact intent before it is signed; an outcome that stays unresolved visibly blocks that account instead of allowing a blind retry. The permissionless recovery calls remain available indefinitely if automation fails.

### Incidents

During an incident the organization may stop normal actions in its frontend and application programming interface (API) and disable opener orchestration. That stop cannot prevent a technical user from calling the contract directly, and it does not stop an existing loan's funding, repayment, refund or release. Someone holding only the stolen opener key can pay to create junk records under invented loan identifiers, which the application quarantines because they are absent from its committed registry, but cannot alter existing records or move anyone's funds. A lost or compromised opener is replaced only through the delayed upgrade path described under authority boundaries.

> **Open question: is there a pause on the contract?**
> The governance selection, the opening-authority constraint and the contract responsibility say this version has no on-chain pause and must not add one; incident response is the frontend, API and opener stop described above. The deployment state record lists pause and operational roles in each deployment's identity and a "paused for new or risky action" state, entered when an authorized operational role triggers a narrow pause, and the opener and pause invariant limits any such role. All sides agree a pause can never move funds, change recipients or terms, or reveal secrets. Whether that pause state is the frontend and API stop or an on-chain role is undecided.

A defect in the contract code itself is a different matter: it could steal or lock USDC for every loan under the deployment, and Bitcoin's fixed transactions cannot repair it. The design requires invariant, adversarial and state-machine testing plus audits before funded use; see the [contract exploit record](../../system-model/contracts/security/sec-016-base-contract-accounting-or-transition-exploit.md).

## Authority boundaries

The contract decides every USDC movement, and each call has one fixed effect:

| Action | Who may submit it | When | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open a funding window | Only the dedicated opener | After Bitcoin qualification, for a new loan | Creates the record and funding vault; moves no money |
| Finalize funding | Anyone | Before the funding deadline, balance at least the principal | Exactly the principal into the main contract |
| Accept by revealing `X` | Anyone holding the matching `X`; in practice the borrower's Vault | Within the acceptance lifetime | Principal to the borrower's fixed recipient; repayment vault created |
| Return unaccepted principal | Anyone | After the acceptance lifetime, without acceptance | Principal once to the lender's settlement recipient |
| Close expired funding, or sweep the funding vault | Anyone | After an unfinalized deadline, or after finalization or expiry | Vault USDC to the lender's settlement recipient |
| Finalize repayment | Anyone | No later than `D`, balance at least the debt | Exactly the debt into the main contract |
| Claim by revealing `Y` | Anyone holding the matching `Y`; in practice the lender's Vault | After valid repayment, strictly before `C` | Debt once to the lender's settlement recipient |
| Switch the refund recipient | Only the borrower's committed protocol address, once | Strictly before `C` | Recipient becomes that same protocol address |
| Refund unclaimed repayment | Anyone | At or after `C`, repayment unclaimed | Debt once to the frozen refund recipient |
| Sweep the repayment vault | Anyone | After finalization or strictly after `D` | Vault USDC to the current or frozen refund recipient |

Around the contract, each participant's authority stays narrow:

- **The lender** controls only its own capital: whether, when and from where to fund. Approving funding grants the product no custody.
- **Payers** gain nothing by paying. A participant's Vault can also hold a separate Base payment account, independent of the protocol address and the Bitcoin keys, whose address is shown only after the participant can restore it from backup. From it, once its payment signing is built and verified (it is planned and not yet verified), the participant can make exact, Vault-approved USDC funding or repayment transfers, and a narrow standalone USDC transfer to an address of their choosing. To the contract it is one more payer: it does not become a protocol caller, change any recipient or alter any existing loan's terms, and a standalone transfer has no loan binding and never advances loan state. A fresh loan may use such an address as one of its existing recipient fields only through the normal approvals.
- **The application** may submit fixed-effect calls and schedule them, but gains no exclusive right, no choice of amount or recipient, and no secret.
- **A workforce operator**, authorized under the [operator constraint](../../build/records/constraints/cons-019-workforce-operator-authorization-and-audit.md), may request only an already-fixed application operation. The operator cannot choose or change the contract, amount, recipient or call effect, and cannot declare an outcome; only final events establish one.
- **Governance** controls upgrades of the main contract, not individual loans. The governance selection has one dedicated key own upgrades during local testing and an intentionally short public beta, after which that same sole ownership moves to a 2-of-3 multisig (a Safe) controlling a timelock. Other records state that this version keeps the Safe-controlled timelock, without mentioning the beta key, and forbid an unrestricted single upgrade key; whether the beta key is a permitted stage or a contradiction is an open question (see below). Under the Safe, before an upgrade can be scheduled, its exact code must be public and pass an automated compatibility check, and every scheduled upgrade waits seven days in public. An approved upgrade becomes the one code path for every loan the contract holds, with no per-loan legacy branch; it must keep existing loan data compatible and preserve fixed recipients, lifecycle authority and each loan's originating identity, and existing loans stay on their originating contract rather than being migrated. Retiring a deployment for new loans leaves existing loans and their routes on it. There is no separate emergency upgrade key, and the opener has no direct setter. The vaults themselves can never be upgraded.

> **What upgradeability means for the noncustodial claim**
> The claim that the company cannot redirect Base funds is only as strong as the administration model. A compromised or malicious upgrade applies to funds the contract holds, and rotating keys afterwards does not reverse a theft. The multisig, the public delay and the compatibility checks are the controls once ownership has moved to the Safe, which has not happened yet. While a single beta key owns upgrades, as the governance selection describes, compromise of that one key would expose every USDC balance the contract holds; whether that stage is permitted is an open question; the [upgrade-compromise record](../../system-model/contracts/security/sec-017-base-upgrade-administrator-or-operational-signer-compromise.md) describes the threat.

The domain must not decide or imply:

- anything on Bitcoin: signing, broadcast, confirmation, the beneficiary of the collateral, or whether the escrow is active;
- that a transfer, balance, simulation, worker result, submitted transaction, unfinalized event, missing `Y` or lender silence means funding, repayment, a claim, a refund, Bitcoin recovery or default;
- custody, pooling or automatic deployment of lender capital, liquidation of collateral, or automatic settlement after default;
- any arbitrary recipient change, general token rescue or discretionary transfer;
- what an observation is worth: finality belongs to the observation domain.

## Choices and their reasons

- **A deployed vault for each loan's funding.** A direct funding call would need the lender's wallet or treasury to build contract calls and approve a pull; an address computed in advance but not yet deployed could strand funds if deployment failed; a shared deposit address would need off-chain attribution. A deployed per-loan vault costs one small contract per opened loan and gives every ordinary transfer an unambiguous destination without memos or sender identity. The user specified a per-loan vault that anyone can fund from any wallet; the vault's exact bindings and recovery rules were approved on the agent's recommendation, and tying funded state to the final event was the agent's choice. The [funding-vault selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-021-immutable-per-loan-funding-vault.md) records the options.
- **A separate repayment vault created at acceptance.** Approve-and-repay would need payer calls and give the contract a token allowance; deploying the repayment vault at opening would spend gas on loans never accepted; one configurable vault type would merge two different recovery policies. Creating it inside acceptance avoids a later deployment race, because every effect succeeds or reverts together. The user specified replacing approve-and-repay with this vault and sending recovered USDC only to the borrower's current refund recipient; the atomic creation and finalization rules were approved on the agent's recommendation, and limiting sweeps to after finalization or after `D` was the agent's choice. See the [repayment-vault selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-022-immutable-per-loan-repayment-vault.md).
- **One explicit function per transition.** A generic entry point would shrink the contract but push type and state selection into callers and weaken audit; one record holding every fact would add storage cost and coupling. Explicit calls make recipients, amounts, deadlines and secret rules visible. This was approved on the agent's recommendation; fitting the later vault and switch transitions into it was the agent's choice. See the [transition interface selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-008-base-contract-transition-interface.md).
- **A 48-hour claim window, then a permanent refund.** The user rejected keeping valid repayment locked indefinitely, judging that holding the USDC there was the danger, and specified 48 hours for the lender's claim followed by a borrower refund that never expires. Setting `T` at 72 hours, which leaves a nominal 24-hour Bitcoin buffer after the latest normal claim, and having the worker schedule the refund, were approved on the agent's recommendation; keeping the recipient switch available until `C` regardless of repayment or claim was the agent's choice. See the [unclaimed-repayment constraint](../../build/records/constraints/cons-010-unclaimed-repayment-exclusivity.md).
- **An upgradeable contract under delayed multisig governance.** Immutable code from day one would leave no repair route during the early beta; a permanently upgradeable proxy leaves a continuing ability to change behavior. The chosen path keeps a governed repair route with public notice and compatibility evidence. The user specified starting under one key for local testing and the short beta (whether that stage fits the whole-version statements is the open question below), moving sole ownership to a multisig, applying upgrades as one code path for every loan, and keeping ownership throughout this version; the proxy mechanism, the 2-of-3 Safe, the seven-day delay and the compatibility checks were approved on the agent's recommendation. The same user decision records incident response as a frontend and API stop with no on-chain pause; the deployment state record's pause state is the open question described under exceptions. See the [governance selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-009-base-contract-upgrade-governance.md).
- **A narrow opener replaced only by upgrade.** The user specified that the opener has no direct setter or emergency role and is replaced only through the delayed upgrade path; one dedicated platform-funded opener that submits only after qualification was approved on the agent's recommendation. See the [opening-authority constraint](../../build/records/constraints/cons-008-base-window-opening-authority.md).
- **Participants sign their own reveals.** The user specified that each participant's protocol address signs and submits acceptance and claim, with the platform sending that address fee money once, with no refill and no daily cap, and with self-funding always allowed, and asked for an internal warning and a hard stop set in ETH; the sizing (the estimated fee plus 50 percent, a warning above 0.00025 ETH and nothing sent above 0.001 ETH) was approved on the agent's recommendation. See the [gas sponsorship contract](../../system-model/contracts/interfaces/iface-015-participant-gas-sponsorship.md).
- **A 24-hour acceptance lifetime.** The user lengthened it from three hours to 24. The record of that change notes, as the agent's addition, that the longer lifetime also lengthens the window in which a stolen `X` could force acceptance, so strict isolation of `X` remains required.

## Open, excluded and deferred

**Open.** Whether the deployment's pause state is the frontend and API stop or an on-chain pause role is undecided, as described under exceptions: the governance selection, the opening-authority constraint and the contract responsibility say there is no on-chain pause, while the deployment state record and its invariant describe a narrow pause state set by an operational role. Whether the single upgrade key for local testing and the short public beta is a permitted stage of this version is also undecided: the governance selection describes it, while the upgrade-boundary invariant, the contract responsibility and the immutable-conversion scope record say this version keeps the 2-of-3 Safe-controlled seven-day timelock without mentioning it, and the deployment state record forbids an unrestricted single upgrade key. The domain has no unresolved tickets.

**Status.** The handoff index records the Base contract delivery as implemented in an earlier delivery, with local verification; a later delta for the local demonstration is recorded as incomplete. Public deployment, explorer verification and an authoritative deployment manifest wait for a later release gate, and moving upgrade ownership from the beta key to the Safe-controlled timelock waits for a later operation gate. No testnet deployment, mainnet deployment or production operation is claimed; see the [runtime claims boundary](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-013-runtime-profile-claims-and-activation-gates.md) and the [handoff index](../../build/workflow/handoffs/README.md). The Vault payment account is a selected design whose payment-signing profile its [selection](../../build/records/selections/sel-043-vault-owned-participant-payment-account-and-local-realization.md) records as planned and not yet verified.

**Excluded from this version:**

- [public or multi-lender matching, pooled lender capital, product custody and automatic capital deployment](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-011-public-multi-lender-pooling-custody-and-automatic-capital.md);
- [Bitcoin liquidation, automated surplus return and shortfall enforcement](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-012-bitcoin-liquidation-surplus-and-shortfall.md);
- recovery of tokens other than the configured USDC.

**Deferred, each outside the current scope until its revisit condition is met:**

- [Base accounting for cooperative settlement](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-005-cooperative-settlement-operations.md): the participants can authorize a cooperative Bitcoin spend, but no Base ledger action records it, so the product may not claim that it closes the Base ledger, and no Base effect follows from it automatically;
- [immutable conversion of the Base contract](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-007-immutable-base-contract-conversion.md): this version keeps upgrade ownership, and renouncing it, including how a new opener would be provided afterwards, needs a separate decision;
- [platform-paid reveal execution](../../system-model/contracts/scope/scope-014-platform-paid-base-reveal-execution.md): no relayer or paymaster signs or pays for acceptance or claim;
- production fee funding for Vault payment accounts: the [one-time stipend](../../system-model/contracts/interfaces/iface-015-participant-gas-sponsorship.md) goes only to protocol addresses, and only the local demonstration provisions payment accounts, with disposable local funds.

The [domain record](../../system-model/domains/base-usdc-settlement-and-funding/domain.md) lists exactly what the domain owns and excludes, and the [Base transition contract](../../system-model/contracts/interfaces/iface-009-base-transition-api-events-and-errors.md) holds every call, event and error.
